# Global Context

Ten-plus years outside the comfort zone — living and working across cultures has shaped how I lead, communicate, and build.

## A Decade in Mexico

I moved to Guadalajara, Mexico in 2014. What started as a professional adventure became home. Guadalajara is Mexico's Silicon Valley — a serious tech hub with a strong talent pool and a very different pace of thinking than I was used to.

Living here gave me a ground-level perspective on what "global" actually means: not just video calls across time zones, but genuinely different ways of approaching problems, building trust, and measuring success. That perspective has become one of my most useful professional assets.

## Working Across Cultures

My day-to-day has involved bridging three worlds simultaneously:

**India** — where I'm from, and where a significant part of my engineering teams are based. Strong technical depth, direct communication style on technical matters, high context in documentation.

**Mexico** — where I live and where local teams operate. Relationship-first culture, high value placed on personal connection before business, strong creative problem-solving.

**USA** — my primary client base. Results-oriented, time-sensitive, prefers direct answers with supporting detail. Trust is built through consistent delivery, not just rapport.

Navigating all three simultaneously, in the same week, often on the same day — that's the job. It's taught me to read a room quickly, adapt my communication register, and find the common ground where diverse teams actually ship things together.

## Front-Facing for US Clients

For the past several years I've been the primary interface between engineering teams in Mexico/India and enterprise clients in the United States. That means:

- Translating technical complexity into business language for C-suite stakeholders
- Running discovery sessions and requirements workshops across cultures
- Setting expectations honestly and holding to delivery commitments
- Being the person who gets the 9pm Slack message and knows how to respond

I've found that US clients value two things above all: reliability and candor. If you say it's going to be done Thursday, it needs to be done Thursday — or you need to communicate early and clearly about why it won't be.
